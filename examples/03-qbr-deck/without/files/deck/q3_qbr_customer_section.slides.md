- Customer: Q3 2026

*Speaker notes:* This section is about one thing: SMB. Revenue grew well this quarter, but almost all of it came from enterprise and mid-market. *Speaker notes:* Total MRR went from $812.6K in June to $878.5K in September, up $65.9K. Enterprise was $48K of that. SMB added about $2K, despite 450 net new SMB customers. That works out to roughly $5 of MRR per net new customer, which doesn't add up: either new customers are on much smaller plans, the ones leaving were bigger, or existing customers are downgrading. We don't have that breakdown yet; flag it as an open question.

## Slide 6

- Strong link
- Months with slower support had more churn (correlation 0.99).
- Not yet proof
- Six data points, and both numbers grew over time. Something else could be driving both.
- To confirm
- Check churned accounts' last-90-day ticket history and exit reasons.
- 6

*Speaker notes:* Each dot is a month. The relationship is almost a straight line. I want to be careful here: it's six months, and both lines are trending up together, so this is a strong hypothesis, not proof. The fastest way to confirm is to pull ticket history for the SMB accounts that churned in Q3 and see if they had slow or unresolved tickets before they left.

*Speaker notes:* Three asks. The first is the real decision: SMB support capacity. If the churn link holds, every month at 14-hour response costs us roughly 100 SMB customers. The second is how we confirm the link quickly, before committing a lot of money. The third is a reporting change, so a strong gross number can't hide a flat net number again.
